LitBench: benchmarking evidence synthesis in epilepsy

LitBench evaluates whether AI systems can find the right papers, identify supporting passages, combine evidence across studies, and recognize when the evidence needed to answer is absent. The benchmark focuses on epilepsy research.

Research question

Can an AI research assistant find and synthesize the right evidence across a challenging medical literature corpus?

Approach and evidence

The medRxiv preprint describes 1,980 open-access papers and 9,472 human-reviewed facts, with single-paper and two-paper questions and tests where the supporting source is deliberately removed.

The authors report that no system was reliable across every tested dimension. More competing papers and cross-paper synthesis created challenges, and systems differed in their willingness to abstain when evidence was missing.

Authors and contributions

Alon Gorenshtein is first author, with Tianyu Zhang, M. Brandon Westover, and Daniel M. Goldenholz. The code is maintained in the Goldenholz Lab repository.

Research context

The linked 2026 manuscript is a preprint and has not been peer reviewed. Results reflect the models and conditions described in that manuscript.
